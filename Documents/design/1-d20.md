# 1.d20 — Concept

**Status:** rough concept, not yet reviewed with Kofi or Amara. Distilled from the d20 Modern SRD
in `Documents/Specs/d20/Modern/` — that folder is the source of truth for rules text; this doc is
about what we're keeping, what we're cutting, and why. Buildable spec now drafted:
[`../Specs/1-d20/1-d20-spec.md`](../Specs/1-d20/1-d20-spec.md) (+
[`1-d20-flavor.md`](../Specs/1-d20/1-d20-flavor.md)).

**Roadmap:** [Phase 2.5 — d20 vertical slice & mechanic infrastructure](../Roadmap/phase-2-5.md).
Rules here need to be authored through `GeneratorServices`/`RulePrototype`, not hand-authored
`ARule` classes the way Nine Rooms was — that's the condition under which this phase also closes
out the original Phase 3 done-bar. See that phase doc before writing the buildable spec.

## Premise

A single-player, GM-less mini-campaign: one player, one pregenerated character, no human running
the table. Everything the SRD would normally leave to GM judgment has to resolve through a fact,
a rule, or a mechanic instead, or it doesn't belong in this slice.

Borrowed, not invented: the SRD ships its own reusable premise, **Department-7** ("a fictional
elite organization... deals with situations threatening the modern world"), explicitly offered as
"an example... providing a common feel for the game." Using it means we're not inventing setting
fiction on top of everything else this slice already has to solve.

**Play level, not fun level.** "Suitable for a 10-year-old" is a complexity ceiling — small
numbers, few moving parts, no rules-lawyering required — not a mandate to optimize for delight.
Session target: a single sitting, on the order of a couple hours. No character progression; the
character that starts the session is the character that finishes it.

## Shape: Nine Rooms' successor

**Reused wholesale, not just in spirit:** the same 3×3 grid, the same row-major room ids, the same
eight win lines (`nine-rooms-facts.md`'s grid layout, adjacency rules, and win-line list —
`[1,2,3][4,5,6][7,8,9]` rows, `[1,4,7][2,5,8][3,6,9]` columns, `[1,5,9][3,5,7]` diagonals). Player
starts in room 5, same as Nine Rooms. "Lovingly arbitrary, yet deterministically refined" — the
tic-tac-toe geometry earns its keep a second time instead of being reinvented.

What changes is what resolves a room's challenge. Nine Rooms used a string-match `Answer` handler;
here, each room's obstacle resolves through a real d20 mechanic instead — a skill check against a
DC, or the single **fight** action against a monster — and the room only counts as solved (feeding
the same `RoomSolved`/`WinMarker` win-line machinery Nine Rooms already has) based on that result.

This reuses a shape Baraka's already built (command handlers, per-room state, the win-line rules,
the win-line-reachability safeguard) and spends the new work on the part that's actually new: real
mechanical resolution in place of a literal-string match.

### Room map

Rooms **1, 5, and 8** are the `fight` encounter; the remaining six (2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9) are skill
checks. Exact DCs, flavor text, and the monster stat block are still open — see
[Next steps](#next-steps).

Checked against the eight win lines the same way `nine-rooms-facts.md`'s reachability safeguard
checks room content — deliberately, not by luck:

```
rows: [1,2,3]* [4,5,6]* [7,8,9]*        (* = touches a combat room)
cols: [1,4,7]* [2,5,8]** [3,6,9]        (** = touches two combat rooms)
diag: [1,5,9]** [3,5,7]*
```

Seven of eight lines require at least one fight; **column 3 (`[3,6,9]`) is combat-free** — a real,
checkable path to a win without fighting at all, per the ask. Column 2 and the `[1,5,9]` diagonal
each route through two combat rooms, so they're the "you committed to fighting" lines. Worth
carrying this same reachability check into the buildable spec once room content is final, the same
way Nine Rooms' `WinLineCatalog` does — not just asserted here and trusted forever.

The six skill rooms, drawn from the real `msrdskillsi.rtf`/`msrdskillsii.rtf` skill list rather
than invented names, favoring physical/practical skills a no-GM game can resolve as a pure number
(nothing Bluff/Diplomacy-shaped — those need a human to judge the fiction, same reason Wealth and
Reputation got cut). DCs are pulled low relative to the SRD's own ladder (`Average` = 10, `Tough` =
15, etc.) on purpose: that ladder assumes a character who's accumulated skill ranks over levels,
and 1.d20 has no ranks at all, just a flat ability modifier — keeping every check in a roughly
45–80% success band at these DCs is a deliberate recalibration, not the SRD's sticker price:

| Room | Skill (ability) | DC | Modifier | Success | Flavor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Search (Int) | 10 | +0 | 55% | Find the latch hidden behind a loose panel. |
| 3 | Climb (Str) | 12 | +3 | 60% | Scale a fence blocking the only way through. |
| 4 | Listen (Wis) | 8 | +0 | 65% | Hear the patrol's footsteps before they hear you. |
| 6 | Swim (Str) | 10 | +3 | 70% | Cross a flooded service corridor. |
| 7 | Disable Device (Int) | 12 | +0 | 45% | Bypass a simple mechanical lock. |
| 9 | Jump (Str) | 8 | +3 | 80% | Leap a gap in a collapsed floor. |

Unlike combat, a failed skill check has no clock and no penalty — same as Nine Rooms' `Answer`
rooms, just re-issue the check. The 3-round clock exists specifically because combat is a
multi-step attrition process that needs a termination guarantee; a single pass/fail skill check
already terminates every time it's attempted, so it doesn't need one.

### Combat: `fight`, `flee`, and a hard clock

Two combat actions, both without nuance:

- **`fight`** — one attack-resolution round: attack roll vs. Defense, damage on a hit, per the
  System attack mechanic. Repeat by issuing it again.
- **`flee`** — auto-success escape, no roll. Ends the encounter immediately and returns the PC to
  the previous room; the monster isn't defeated, but the room isn't permanently failed either — the
  PC can walk back in and start a fresh fight later. Exists mainly so a fight can be deterministically
  exited in a test transcript without depending on combat RNG.

**Three-round clock:** if the monster isn't Down within 3 rounds of `fight`, it flees on its own —
distinct from the PC choosing to `flee`. When that happens, the room is **permanently unsolved for
the rest of the session**: the monster fact is gone, there's nothing left to fight, and
`RoomSolved` can never fire for that room again. Any win line through it is dead for the rest of
that playthrough (the other lines aren't affected). This is a deliberate runtime failure mode, not
a content bug — a different thing from the *authoring-time* reachability check above, which only
guarantees a line is winnable in principle, not that a given playthrough won't fail it. Combat
resolves to exactly one of three outcomes — PC wins, PC goes Down, or the clock runs out — none of
them open-ended, which is the point: bounded enough for Lothar to write a finite transcript against
every branch.

## Scope split: System vs. Scenario

**Updated 2026-07-20** — superseding the split as originally drafted here, per
[Phase 2.6](../Roadmap/phase-2-6.md)'s sharpened dividing line (design discussion with Amara, same
date): **direct system access, not "is the logic generic," is what separates ROM from Rulebook.** A
Rulebook never touches `LunaFlow.Engine` primitives directly — no `ICommandHandler`, no
`LunaFlowEngine.Host`, no `RuleEngine` — regardless of whether its specific logic happens to be
reusable elsewhere. The original version of this section split System/Scenario by genericness
(`check`/`fight`'s math is generic → System; `move`/`look`/`flee` → Scenario) — Phase 2.6 calls that
distinction wrong: writing a command handler at all *is* direct system access, so all five of
1.d20's handlers are ROM-tier, full stop. Confirmed against ticket
[0021](../ClaudeWiki/Processes/Handoffs/0021-phase-2-6-spec-alignment.md), whose fight/flee ruling
predates this sharpening and is superseded by it, not still in force.

Per the ROM/Rulebook distinction in `glossary.md` and `architecture.md`'s "system, setting,
session" framing — System is the reusable d20 chassis (ROM-shaped, no specific game attached),
Scenario is this specific Department-7 mission built on top of it (Rulebook-shaped, pure data once
Phase 2.6 lands — no handler code, no rule code, nothing that reaches `LunaFlow.Engine` even
transitively).

**System (ROM) keeps:**

- Six ability scores and the modifier formula (`(score / 2) − 5`, rounded down).
- The universal resolution rule: `d20 + modifiers vs. target number`.
- Defense (`10 + Dex mod + class bonus + equipment bonus + size mod`, size mod fixed at +0 for all
  v1 content — no non-Medium creatures yet).
- Saving throws (Fortitude/Reflex/Will = base save bonus + ability mod vs. a DC set by the effect).
- Skill checks (`1d20 + skill modifier`) — skill *modifiers* are fixed values on a pregenerated
  sheet; the skill-point economy (ranks, class skill lists, cross-class buying) is not modeled.
- Attack resolution (melee/ranged attack roll vs. Defense, damage on a hit).
- The Basic Class *math* is class-agnostic (ability-score-keyed modifiers apply the same way
  regardless of which class owns them) — but v1 only ever instantiates one: **Strong**, the
  STR-keyed archetype. The other five (Fast, Tough, Smart, Dedicated, Charismatic) aren't needed
  and aren't blocked; System doesn't care which class a future PC picks. No class tables, no
  per-level progression — the pregenerated character's numbers are fixed at whatever level-1
  Strong implies, not derived from a table at runtime.
- A softened condition set: SRD `Dying`/`Disabled`/`Dead` collapse into a single **Down** state for
  v1 (exact trigger/recovery numbers TBD in the buildable spec) — a deliberate content-design call,
  not an inherited default. Implicit in attack resolution, not its own bullet (ruled in 0021).
- **All five command handlers** (`move`, `look`, `check`, `fight`, `flee`), parameterized to read
  Scenario-supplied data rather than authored against Department-7 specifically — not because
  their logic is generic (some of it, like the fight/flee flow, is content-specific pacing), but
  because authoring an `ICommandHandler` is itself system access a Rulebook doesn't get.
- The fact *shapes* the handlers use (`Room`, `CombatantStats`, `CombatRound`, etc.) — Scenario
  only ever supplies instances of them, never new shapes.
- The declarative rule group (win-line rules, `GameEndRule`) — still pipeline-authored.

**Scenario (Rulebook) keeps — data only:** the specific rooms (which are skill checks vs. the fight
encounter, DCs, flavor text), the monster's specific stat values, the pregenerated character's
specific sheet values, and the win condition's specific shape (same win-line completion Nine Rooms
already fires on, expressed as Rulebook-supplied instances of ROM-owned rule shapes).

### The character: one pregenerated Strong-hero fighter

One PC, one archetype, no build choices: STR-keyed Strong hero, level 1. Ability scores are the
"typical fighter" spread — STR and CON high, everything else flat average (10, the SRD's normal-
human baseline, modifier +0) — which also happens to make the math trivial: only two ability
modifiers are ever non-zero.

| Ability | Score | Modifier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| STR | 16 | +3 |
| CON | 16 | +3 |
| DEX | 10 | +0 |
| INT | 10 | +0 |
| WIS | 10 | +0 |
| CHA | 10 | +0 |

Derived from the level-1 Strong Hero row in `msrdbasicclasses.rtf` (BAB +1, Fort +1, Ref +0,
Will +0, class Defense bonus +1, Hit Die 1d8 — 1st-level characters take max HD, not a roll):

| Stat | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HP | 11 | 8 (max 1d8) + 3 (Con) |
| Defense | 11 | 10 + 0 (Dex) + 1 (class) + 0 (equipment) + 0 (size) |
| Melee attack bonus | +4 | +1 (BAB) + 3 (Str) + 0 (size) |
| Damage | 1d8+3 | Longsword ("a sword," `msrdequipmentweaponsandarmor.rtf`) + Str |
| Fortitude save | +4 | +1 (base) + 3 (Con) |
| Reflex save | +0 | +0 (base) + 0 (Dex) |
| Will save | +0 | +0 (base) + 0 (Wis) |

Talents, bonus feats, and the Simple Weapons Proficiency starting feat all exist in the SRD's
level-1 Strong Hero but aren't modeled — no talent trees, no feat system. One deliberate loose end:
a Longsword is actually an *Archaic* weapon in the SRD (needs Archaic Weapons Proficiency, which a
1st-level Strong hero doesn't start with — only Simple). Since Feats-as-a-system is already cut,
1.d20 treats the PC as proficient with their starting weapon by content-design fiat rather than
modeling the −4 nonproficient penalty — sidesteps a feat distinction we already decided not to
build, same call applied to the monster below.

### The monster: a seedy man with a rapier

Not built from the Basic Class tables the way the PC was — just directly authored numbers using
the same System formulas (attack roll vs. Defense, damage on a hit), since he's Scenario flavor
content, not a playable archetype. A Rapier (`msrdequipmentweaponsandarmor.rtf`: 1d6, Piercing,
crit 18–20 — an Archaic weapon, same proficiency fiat as the PC's sword) fits a quick, precise
duelist rather than a brute, which is the deliberate contrast with the PC's STR/CON-heavy sheet:

| Stat | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Defense | 13 |
| Melee attack bonus | +3 |
| Damage | 1d6+1 (rapier) |
| HP | 8 |

Against the PC (attack +4 vs. Defense 13 → hits ~60% of rolls; PC Defense 11 vs. his +3 → he hits
~65%), this trades in the PC's favor but isn't a guaranteed win — the fight can plausibly go either
way inside the 3-round clock, which is the point. Same monster in all three combat rooms (1, 5, 8)
for v1; a second monster type is a content addition for later, not required now.

**Expansion is a content decision, not an engine one.** The `fight` action stays this simple in
Scenario, but the System-level attack mechanic underneath it shouldn't be built as if "one PC, one
weapon, one attack type" is a permanent fact of the world — it should take the inputs it actually
needs (attacker, target, attack bonus, damage) as data, not hard-code them to this PC's numbers.
That way, a future Scenario adding a second weapon, a ranged option, or a second archetype is new
*content* (more facts, more pregenerated sheets) exercising the same mechanic, not a reason to
touch `LunaFlow.Engine` or the mechanic's shape. Worth stating explicitly now so the buildable spec
doesn't accidentally paint the mechanic into "STR fighter" as a special case.

**Cut entirely, not just deferred:**

- **Wealth and Reputation.** Both are structurally GM-dependent in the SRD text itself — "the GM
  decides when a hero's reputation can be relevant," "the GM must decide that a character's fame
  or infamy can come into play." No GM means no mechanism to decide *when* these apply; automating
  that decision would mean inventing rules the SRD doesn't have. Not a scope trim, a fit problem.
- Character creation, leveling, and the skill-point economy — pregenerated sheets sidestep all of
  it for v1.
- Advanced Classes, Occupations, Feats-as-a-system, FX/psionics, vehicles.
- Action Points — mechanically automatable (the "before the result is revealed" timing just means
  the interface delays revealing pass/fail), but adds a level-scaling table this single-level slice
  doesn't need. Candidate for a later pass, not v1.

## Player model and the round/turn seam

Resolved by narrowing scope, not by building new engine machinery: **one player, one PC.** The
existing game-loop model — one player command, one turn, one `Fire()` — stays exactly as-is. d20's
round/initiative structure (multiple combatants acting in turn order) doesn't need to exist in
1.d20 at all, because there's never more than one acting party to sequence against a monster.
Where the SRD's combat text implies a monster's turn, 1.d20 resolves that as a direct consequence
of the player's action within the same command (e.g., a miss triggers the monster's counterattack
in the same `Then()`, rather than as a separately scheduled turn) — the monster's threat is
expressed as facts and rule consequences, not as an independently-acting agent with its own
initiative slot.

Multi-actor scheduling — a real party, multiple monsters each getting independent turns — is
explicitly out of scope here and named for later: **1.x.d20** is where a multi-action scheduler
would get designed, once this slice exists to build on. Not a ticket to Amara today; there's
nothing to ask for yet since v1 doesn't need it.

## Open seams / likely dependencies

- **`IMechanic`** — **update 2026-07-20:** this did get ticketed once the spec made the shape
  concrete, as anticipated. [Ticket 0015](../ClaudeWiki/Processes/Handoffs/0015-imechanic-dice-resolution.md),
  resolved: `IMechanic` grows only `IHasLunaFlowReference` (mirrors `ARule`), not dice/check-resolution
  members directly — those land on a concrete Mechanic class instead, to keep `IMechanic` itself
  general enough for a future non-dice Mechanic (a Physics ROM's trajectory math, e.g.) to implement.
- **ROM/Rulebook loading** stays on the existing hardcoded `RuleEngineOptions.FromAssembly(...)`
  path per `non-goals.md` — no new component model presumed necessary for a System/Scenario split
  that's really just two assemblies (or two namespaces) loaded the same way Nine Rooms is today.
  If that turns out not to hold once we're spec'ing for real, that's the ticket.
- **Where System actually lives** (a new `LunaFlow.Rom` module vs. somewhere else) is a naming/
  project-layout question I'm deliberately not settling here — working name "1.d20 System" until
  Baraka/Amara have an opinion.
